### AI / Models / Agents

[Reward is not the optimization target — LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/pdaGN6pQyQarFHXF4/reward-is-not-the-optimization-target): Explores the distinction between reinforcement learning rewards and the goals a model learns. In an update, the author acknowledges that reward can empirically become a primary goal, though this does not follow by definition.

[AI Alignment Forum](https://www.alignmentforum.org/): A community for AI alignment research and discussion.

[Bayes' rule — LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/w/bayes-rule): An introduction to Bayes' rule and the intuition behind it.

[LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/): Articles and discussions about rationality, cognition, and AI.

[Dario Amodei — We Must Pace the Frontier](https://darioamodei.com/post/we-must-pace-the-frontier): Dario Amodei discusses coordinating the pace of frontier AI development to give safety research and society more time to respond.

[GPT 6 Astra Embodied Policy Evaluation](https://anonymous-report-421.github.io/public-website/?lang=en&view=1): An anonymous evaluation report on GPT-6 Astra's embodied policies.

[Latest research - Goodfire](https://www.goodfire.com/research): Goodfire's research directory, focused on model interpretability and internal mechanisms.

[Augmented Lagrangian Predictive Coding: training 1000-layer networks without backpropagation](https://pub.sakana.ai/pc-alm/): Sakana AI applies an augmented Lagrangian method to predictive coding, exploring ways to train deep networks without backpropagation.

[P(doom) \| Armin Ronacher's Thoughts and Writings](https://lucumr.pocoo.org/2026/9/12/pdoom/): Armin Ronacher reflects on catastrophic AI risk and how people express its probability.

[The Age of Wonders and Terrors | Scott Aaronson](https://scottaaronson.blog/?p=10062): Scott Aaronson discusses the hopes and concerns raised by AI progress.

[Introducing System One Models & Jev | TypeSafe AI](https://typesafe.ai/blog/introducing-system-one-models-and-jev): TypeSafe AI introduces System One models and Jev for structured decision-making.

[From Academia to Alignment | Mike Winer](https://millicosm.substack.com/p/from-academia-to-alignment): Mike Winer shares his experience and reflections on moving from academic research to AI alignment.

[Existential Risk from AI: An Exposition for Mathematicians](https://alkjash.github.io/ai-risk/#preliminaries): An explanation of AI existential risk and alignment for readers with a mathematical background.

[MiMo-v2.6 RL](https://mimo.xiaomi.com/rl/#overview): Xiaomi's introduction to reinforcement learning for MiMo-v2.6.

[Introducing Arrow 2 and Arrow 2 Telos – QuiverAI](https://quiver.ai/blog/introducing-arrow-2-0): QuiverAI introduces the Arrow 2 and Arrow 2 Telos vector graphics generation models.

[Training Search Agents with GRPO — Jasper Lu](https://jasperlu.com/blog/training-search-agents-grpo/): A practical account of training search agents with GRPO.

[Helix 2.5: Zero-Shot 30-Home Generalization](https://www.figure.ai/news/helix-2-5-zero-shot-30-home-generalization): Figure evaluates Helix 2.5 on three household tasks in 30 unseen homes, reporting an overall success rate of 56%; the tasks were fine-tuned in other environments.

[Cache-to-Cache: Direct Semantic Communication Between Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.03215): Research on transferring intermediate semantic representations directly between language models through KV caches, reducing the information loss of text-only communication.

[Pretraining data, not verifiability, is why LLMs are especially good at math (and coding) — LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/xvdngZAqFZfek7KGH/pretraining-data-not-verifiability-is-why-llms-are): Proposes that the structure of pretraining data may explain language models' strengths in mathematics and programming better than the verifiability of answers.

[Reading a Pretraining Run — Chunyuan Deng](https://charlesdddd.github.io/blog/reading-a-pretraining-run.html): A guide to monitoring loss, gradients, activations, and evaluations during pretraining, including metric definitions, sampling methods, and diagnostic priorities.

[Qwen3.8-livetranslate](https://qwen.ai/blog?id=qwen3.8-livetranslate): The official introduction to Qwen3.8-livetranslate.

I compiled these articles into Chinese–English bilingual PDFs, with separate layouts for phones and computers: [Mobile edition (PDF, 103 pages)](/files/week-25/Impossible-Questions-Bilingual-Mobile.pdf) · [Desktop edition (PDF, 56 pages)](/files/week-25/Impossible-Questions-Bilingual.pdf).

- [Finding meaning in the impossible](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/finding-meaning-in-the-impossible/) (editor's introduction): Pursuing seemingly unanswerable questions can lead to unexpected practical discoveries; the journey itself may be meaningful.
- [The questions science may never answer](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-questions-science-may-never-answer/): Science offers approximations that keep evolving. Some questions may remain out of reach because their subjects cannot be observed, or because observation changes them.
- [Will we ever know how life began?](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/will-we-ever-know-how-life-began/): Experiments can reproduce possible paths to life's origins, such as metabolism-first scenarios or an RNA world, but establishing which path actually occurred is much harder.
- [Will we ever achieve immortality?](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/can-humans-live-forever/): Explores slowing aging and cellular reprogramming. Current longevity records do not establish an unbreakable biological limit, though substantially extending life remains a major challenge.
- [What if we never find dark matter?](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/what-if-we-never-find-dark-matter/): Gravitational evidence for dark matter is extensive, but its particle identity has not been directly confirmed. Perhaps science can offer predictions without reaching an absolute truth.
- [The hunt for reality’s smallest pieces](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/what-are-the-smallest-pieces-of-the-universe/): Could strings or quantum units of spacetime lie beneath the Standard Model's 17 particles? Experiments approaching the Planck scale remain almost out of reach.
- [What if ‘consciousness’ isn’t real?](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/what-if-consciousness-isnt-real/): Consciousness may not be a useful scientific category; studying infants, bees, or AI might call for different distinctions.
- [What came before the big bang?](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/what-came-before-the-big-bang-cosmology/): No-boundary models, a big bounce, and a CPT-mirrored universe offer testable predictions, but “before” may be meaningless or impossible to access directly.
- [Why we love unexplainable mysteries](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/why-we-love-unexplainable-mysteries/): An interview with Unexplainable about the appeal of mysteries, from the universe's origins to why bicycles stay upright. The unknown need not be discouraging.
